Introduction

Project goal

The Budget Adjustment Monitor (MAP, from its Spanish name) is a fiscal transparency and analysis tool that quantifies the real impact of the budget adjustment carried out in Argentina since December 2023.

The project was born from the need to measure public spending not only in nominal pesos — which hide the effect of inflation — but in real terms (constant purchasing power) and in US dollars at the historical official exchange rate.

The MAP answers concrete questions: how much purchasing power did the health budget lose? Did pension spending grow or fall in real terms? Which part of the adjustment was a nominal cut and which part was inflationary erosion (licuación)?

Introduction

Scope and limitations

What it covers

The MAP analyzes Argentina's National Budget of the National Executive Branch (PEN), comparing the original 2023 allocation against the current appropriations (crédito vigente) of 2024, 2025 and 2026.

Cut-off point: The baseline is the budget approved for fiscal year 2023 (before the LLA administration took office in December of that year). Changes are measured from that point to the latest available current appropriation.

Important limitations

Experimental tool: The results are algorithmic indicators based on official public data. They imply no value judgment, accusation or finding of responsibility about any jurisdiction, agency or person.

The main technical limitations are:

  • The CPI factor is computed with INDEC data and may use a fallback if the BCRA API is unavailable.
  • The official USD exchange rate may show "N/D" (not available) if the BCRA API is down — in that case an approximate reference value is used.
  • Programs with an original 2023 budget below $100M are excluded from the analysis to avoid statistical distortions.
  • Programs with a real change above +500% or below -99.5% are filtered out as data outliers.
  • Sectors (Health, Pensions, etc.) are defined by jurisdiction name and may overlap when one jurisdiction covers several areas.
Methodology

Key concepts

① Nominal adjustment
Change in the budget appropriation in current pesos. It compares the current amount with the original 2023 amount without correcting for inflation. A positive nominal adjustment does not mean greater purchasing power.
② Real adjustment
Change deflated by the CPI accumulated since January 2023. It measures the change in the real purchasing power of public spending. A negative real adjustment means the State can buy fewer goods and services with that budget.
③ USD adjustment
Change in the budget measured in dollars at the official BNA exchange rate. It compares the USD value of the original budget (at the January 2023 rate) with the current value (at the current rate).
Erosion (licuación)
Difference between the nominal and the real adjustment. It is the part of the adjustment caused by inflationary erosion — spending grew nominally but inflation reduced it in real terms. Erosion = Nominal adjustment − Real adjustment.
Cut (recorte)
When the nominal adjustment is negative — the State spent fewer nominal pesos than in 2023. It is the portion of the adjustment caused by an explicit decision to reduce spending in absolute terms.
CPI factor
Ratio of accumulated inflation from January 2023 to the current period. A factor of 10.53 means prices multiplied 10.53 times. It is used to deflate current amounts to constant 2023 pesos.
Current appropriation (crédito vigente)
The approved budget as modified by every Administrative Decision of the Chief of Cabinet (JGM) that increased or reduced it during the fiscal year. It is the budget actually available at a given moment.
Methodology

Formulas

Nominal adjustment

# Change in current pesos ajuste_nominal_pct = (vigente / original - 1) × 100 # Where: vigente = latest current appropriation (2026 > 2025 > 2024) in millions of pesos original = 2023 allocated budget in millions of pesos

Real adjustment (deflated by CPI)

# Change in constant January 2023 pesos real_pesos = vigente / factor_ipc ajuste_real_pct = (real_pesos / original - 1) × 100 # CPI factor = CPI accumulated from Jan-2023 to today # Example: if prices tripled, factor_ipc = 3.0 # Currently: factor_ipc ≈ 10.53 (inflation ×10.53 since Jan-2023)

Dollar adjustment

# Comparison at the official BNA exchange rate original_usd = original / TC_ene2023 # Jan-2023 rate = $187/USD vigente_usd = vigente / TC_actual # current BNA rate ajuste_usd_pct = (vigente_usd / original_usd - 1) × 100

Erosion

# Portion of the adjustment absorbed by inflation licuacion_pct = ajuste_nominal_pct - ajuste_real_pct # Interpretation: # If nominal = +500% and real = -60% → erosion = 560% # Spending rose nominally but inflation (×10.53) # reduced it in real terms.

Weighted sector average

# Each sector's averages are weighted by original amount # so that small programs do not distort the result total_orig = Σ(monto_original) over all programs in the sector avg_real_ponderado = Σ(variacion_real_pct × monto_original) / total_orig
Why we weight by amount: A program with a $1M budget and a -99% real change should not weigh the same as one with $500,000M and -30%. The weighted average reflects the real economic impact of each program within the sector.

Dashboard

Main tabs

📊
Ranking
Lists the programs with the largest loss of real purchasing power. It shows bars for the nominal adjustment (blue) vs the real adjustment (red/green). Hovering shows amounts in current pesos, USD at the rate of the moment, and the number of budget modifications.
📂
By spending category (inciso)
Aggregates the adjustment by type of spending: Inciso 1 (Personnel), Inciso 2 (Consumer goods), Inciso 3 (Non-personal services), Inciso 4 (Capital goods), Inciso 5 (Transfers), Inciso 7 (Debt service).
🔵
Nominal vs real
Scatter plot where each point is a program. The X axis is the nominal adjustment and the Y axis the real adjustment. Points below the diagonal were eroded by inflation — they rose nominally but fell in real terms.
📋
Line-item detail
Full table with every program. Includes filters by jurisdiction and status. Columns: nominal %, real %, USD %, erosion %, with absolute amounts in current pesos and dollars at the rate of the moment.
💵
Monetary base
Live Monetary Base data from the BCRA public API. Shows its evolution since December 7, 2023 (inauguration day), the accumulated multiplier and the value in dollars at the official BNA rate.
📊
Summary
Consolidated view of every sector with averages weighted by amount. Includes a comparative sector ranking and a chart of the estimated evolution over time.
⚖️
Erosion vs cut
Splits each jurisdiction's adjustment into two components: the part absorbed by inflation (erosion) and the part that was an explicit nominal cut. It shows which ministries adjusted by lowering spending and which ones "let inflation do the adjusting".
Dashboard

Sector tabs

Each sector tab filters programs by jurisdiction and computes KPIs with a weighted average by original amount. The sectors and their source jurisdictions are:

🏥
Health sector
Source: Ministry of Health. Includes public health, disease prevention, patient care and health regulation programs.
👴
Pensions sector
Source: Ministry of Human Capital + Treasury Obligations. Covers ANSES (the social security administration) and pension transfers.
👶
Children sector
Source: Ministry of Human Capital + Ministry of Social Development. Child protection, AUH (Universal Child Allowance) and family programs.
🏗️
Public works sector
Source: Ministry of Public Works + Ministry of Infrastructure + Ministry of Transport.
💼
Public wages sector
Source: All ministries, filtered by Inciso 1 (Personnel). Measures the real evolution of State payroll spending across every jurisdiction.
📚
Education sector
Source: Ministry of Education + Ministry of Human Capital. Includes transfers to universities, scholarships and national education programs.
Sector overlap: The Ministry of Human Capital concentrates pensions, children and education. As a result some programs appear in more than one sector. This is a limitation of the current data model that will be refined.
Dashboard

Header KPIs

Programs analyzed
Total budget programs with valid data in the 2023 base and a current appropriation available in at least one later fiscal year. Excludes token budgets and outliers.
Average real adjustment
Simple average of the real change of every program with a reduction. It indicates the average fall in budget purchasing power since January 2023.
Accumulated CPI factor
Inflation accumulated since January 2023 expressed as a multiplier. A factor of 10.53× means prices multiplied 10.53 times — that is, +953% accumulated inflation.
Programs with >50% real reduction
Number of programs whose real budget fell more than 50% compared with 2023. It measures the depth of the adjustment — what share of the State lost more than half of its spending capacity.

Data

Data updates

1
Budget: The 2026 current appropriation is updated every day by the Daily Budget Monitor workflow, which downloads the Presupuesto Abierto dataset (Ministry of Economy). Real figures use an annual average price deflator (INDEC CPI via BCRA) and compare current appropriation against current appropriation.
2
CPI and exchange rate: They are queried in real time from the BCRA public API every time the dashboard loads. If the API is down, the system uses fallback values computed from INDEC data.
3
Monetary Base: Queried live from the BCRA when opening the "Monetary base" tab. The data is daily with a one-business-day lag.
4
Social indicators: Automatic GitHub Action on the 1st and 15th of each month at 06:00 AM Argentina time. It detects changes in the DEIS and SNIC pages and updates the data JSON.

Glossary

ARS
Argentine peso. Currency in which the national budget is denominated.
BCRA
Central Bank of the Argentine Republic. Source of CPI, exchange rate and Monetary Base data.
Original appropriation
Amount budgeted at the start of the fiscal year, before any modification.
Current appropriation
Amount budgeted after every modification during the fiscal year (JGM Administrative Decisions).
DA / JGM
Administrative Decision of the Chief of Cabinet of Ministers. Legal instrument that modifies the budget during the fiscal year.
DEIS
Directorate of Health Statistics and Information. Ministry of Health agency that publishes the vital statistics yearbooks.
Deflate
Divide a nominal value by the price index to obtain its value in constant currency (real purchasing power).
CPI factor
Accumulated CPI expressed as a multiplier. Factor 10.53 = accumulated inflation of 953%.
Inciso
Classification of spending by object. Inciso 1=Personnel, 2=Consumer goods, 3=Non-personal services, 4=Capital goods, 5=Transfers, 7=Debt service.
Erosion (licuación)
Loss of purchasing power due to inflation. Spending rises nominally but falls in real terms.
MM / B
Abbreviations for millions (MM) and thousands of millions/billions (B) of pesos.
PEN
National Executive Branch. The budget analyzed covers every PEN jurisdiction.
SNIC
National Criminal Information System. Part of the Ministry of Security, it publishes crime and suicide statistics.
TC BNA
Official exchange rate of Banco de la Nación Argentina (selling). Used to convert budgets into dollars.
